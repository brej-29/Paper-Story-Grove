# Replacement story direction and cultural checks

These three stories are newly written fiction. They are not retellings of traditional tales. The original Ammu, Meenu, and Puff stories remain separate and unchanged. These research notes belong in project documentation, not in the children's reading flow or the grown-ups modal.

## Why these replacements are distinct

| Story | Concrete story problem | Child's decision | Difference from the first three |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anu and the Last Little Lamp | A neighbour returns after dark during Karthigai Deepam; Anu wants her to feel expected. | A visible doorway welcome or a courtyard place and personal call. | Domestic festival lamps, anticipation, and welcoming a specific person; no kolam, Pongal, boat trip, or cloud quest. |
| Dev and the Extra Banana Leaf | A cousin arrives early for Onam sadya, and the prepared row has no space. | Extend one row or arrange two facing rows with a serving aisle. | A real meal, spatial problem-solving, and family humour; no rain-listening, river travel, or floral-pattern activity. |
| Moss and the Kite That Sneezed | A dragon repairer accidentally tears a customer's kite and takes responsibility. | Repair it as a ribbon comet or a broad-winged bird. | Workshop comedy, making and testing, and repair after a mistake; no shadows or bedtime cloud search. |

Each story has four shared pages and two two-page branches: six pages per reading path, eight page objects, and two endings. Neither branch punishes the reader. Scenes use the independent `Illustration.scene` renderer, rather than expanding the original paper-cut art helpers.

## Tamil story: verified setting, invented household

Karthigai Deepam is associated with Tamil Nadu and the lighting of small clay lamps around homes in the evening. This is the specific practice used in the story. The plot, characters, spare lamp, returning neighbour, and welcome choices are invented. No claim is made that reserving a lamp for a traveller is a festival custom. The text does not reduce the festival's religious meanings to an invented universal moral.

Source: Government of India's [Incredible India: Karthigai Deepam](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/karthigai-deepam), consulted 16 November 2026. Only the home lamp-lighting setting is used; dates, legends, and detailed ritual claims are deliberately outside this story's scope.

Anu's grandmother fills, carries, places, and lights the lamps. Anu chooses, watches, waves, calls, and unrolls a mat. The courtyard lamp stands on a clear ledge away from clothing and food. Illustrations should preserve those roles and must not show Anu carrying or lighting a burning lamp. The story introduces “Paatti” as Anu's grandmother; “aunty” is an affectionate form of address to the adult neighbour, not a genealogical claim.

## Kerala story: sadya structure matters

The Onam sadya provides more than scenery: the meal requires a place and banana leaf for each diner, and clear space for serving. The leaf's tapering end points to the seated diner's left. Side dishes and savouries precede rice; rice occupies the leaf's lower portion. The story includes sambar, banana chips, pappadam, and sweet payasam. It does not prescribe an exact dish count or claim one arrangement describes every household.

Sources consulted 16 November 2026:

- [Kerala Tourism: Onasadhya — A Feast of Delicacies](https://www.keralatourism.org/kerala-article/2015/onasadya-kerala/553) supports the vegetarian lunch setting, banana leaf orientation, rice placement, and sides-before-rice serving order.
- [Kerala Tourism: Highlights of Onam](https://www.keralatourism.org/faq/what-are-the-highlights-of-onam-celebration) supports Onam sadya and the named sambar and payasam dishes.
- [Kottayam District, Government of Kerala: Sadhya](https://kottayam.nic.in/en/culinary-delight/sadhya/) supports banana chips and pappadam among the meal's components.

The late-arriving cousin and seating puzzle are fictional. Everyone moves mats and empty leaves before serving; nobody shuffles a laden leaf. Both plans preserve serving access. In facing rows, each leaf's orientation is relative to its own diner, so the two rows should not blindly reuse the same leaf orientation. The story describes this family's floor seating, not a universal requirement or an exclusion of people who use chairs.

## English fantasy and repair logic

Moss is a small dragon with dexterous claws and a tail, not a renamed human. A sneeze sends the kite onto a hook; no flame causes the tear. Moss admits the mistake, and Mouse helps choose the repair. Both endings follow a patch-and-dry step before flight. The bird branch adds lightweight frame support for the new wings. These are fantastical story events, not a technical kite-making tutorial.

## Illustration and interaction review criteria

- Show the named characters and event for each stage, rather than repeating a generic landscape.
- Distinguish branch outcomes visually: doorway/courtyard; one row/two rows; comet/bird.
- Keep lamps adult-handled, food serving orderly, and dragon breath non-flaming.
- Each illustration exposes three meaningful discoveries tied to what is happening on that page.
- Keep cultural labels grounded in the action; do not add an instructional glossary or factual overlay to the reading flow.
- Preserve the original stories and their original art byte-for-byte.

Static route checks confirm six-page paths and two endings per replacement. Visual composition and interaction quality still require browser inspection of the completed renderer.
